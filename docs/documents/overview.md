# Documents & PDFs

GearFlow generates professional PDF documents directly from your projects — quotes, invoices, delivery dockets, return sheets, pull slips, call sheets, and timelines. Every document pulls live data straight from the project, so the gear list, crew schedule, dates, addresses, and pricing are always current and you never type the same thing twice.

That matters because these documents are how your business shows up to the outside world. The quote a venue client receives, the docket your driver hands the stage manager at the dock, the call sheet your audio tech checks at 6am — all of it carries your branding and all of it has to be right. Generating from project data means a last-minute gear swap or an added day on the run shows up on the next document automatically, with no risk of a stale spreadsheet going out the door.

## What gets generated

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Quote** | Financial summary with grouped line items, discount, tax, and total |
| **Invoice** | Like a quote, plus tax ID, payment terms, deposit and balance |
| **Delivery Docket** | Checked-out equipment with checkboxes and signature lines for the dock |
| **Return Sheet** | Condition-tracking sheet for gear coming back from site |
| **Pull Slip / Packing List** | Warehouse pick list with per-unit checkboxes and asset tags |
| **Call Sheet** | Crew schedule with call times, roles, and contact details |
| **Timeline** | Date-grouped run of every service — deliveries, bump-in/out, pickups |

## Generating a document

Open any project and click the **Documents** button in the top bar, then choose the document type from the dropdown.

If you've published custom templates for that document type, they appear in a sub-menu alongside **Default** — pick the one you want. If you haven't customised anything, GearFlow uses your organisation's default template, falling back to the built-in system default.

GearFlow assembles the PDF from live project data and opens it in a new tab for you to review before it goes anywhere.

## How documents read your project

Documents aren't a flat dump of every line item — they restructure the project to suit their audience:

- **Client-facing documents** (quotes, invoices) **collapse** project groups into a single billable line. The client sees "Lighting Package x1 — $5,000", not every individual lamp and clamp inside it.
- **Warehouse and on-site documents** (pull slips, delivery dockets, return sheets) **expand** every group down to the gear, organised by category and sorted in packer order (by location, then category, then model) so the crew can walk the shelves in one pass.
- **Sub-hired gear** pulls into its own section at the bottom, grouped by supplier, so hired-in items are clearly separated from your own stock.

Delivery dockets and return sheets are also **deployment-aware**: a delivery docket only lists gear that's actually **checked out**, so if nothing's been deployed yet the docket comes back empty — deploy from the warehouse first.

## Downloading and sending

Once the PDF is open you can:

- **Download** — save the PDF to your device
- **Print** — print directly from the browser

Your company logo, colours, and footer are applied automatically from the template's brand settings.

## Templates and branding

Every document type uses a **template** that controls its layout, sections, visibility rules, and branding. Templates are built from stackable sections — header, client details, equipment table, totals, signatures, and more — and link to a reusable **brand template** so all your documents share one consistent look.
